# Directory scope, seed sources and verification methods for Job Board Atlas

Research date: 2026-11-12. Scope: global. Notes written without em dashes; citations follow each finding after a hyphen.

## 1. How many organisations exist in each category?

### Takeaway
No source publishes a definitive census for any category. The defensible order of magnitude is low thousands of job boards worldwide (3,000 to 7,000 depending on definition), about 27,000 staffing firms in the US alone, a top 100 global staffing list that covers about 40% of the market, roughly 75 to 80+ national public employment services, and a fragmented ATS market where analysts profile only 15 to 25 vendors. Against these numbers, Job Board Atlas's roughly 600 entries cover a meaningful slice of job boards but only a tiny fraction of staffing firms.

### Cited Findings

**Job boards and aggregators**
- SIA has cited "something in the region of 3,500 job boards globally", tied to a 2016 market figure, so it is dated. - [SIA Online Job Advertising Market](https://www.staffingindustry.com/Research/Research-Reports/APAC/Online-Job-Advertising-Market)
- Jobboard Finder (a job board directory) has said its team "references more than 3,000 active job boards in the world" (undated post, probably around 2014). - [Jobboard Finder news](https://www.jobboardfinder.com/news/?p=123)
- Jobboard Finder figures conflict by date: 400 board deals in 2016 ([Recruiter](https://www.recruiter.co.uk/news/2016/01/jobboard-finder-signs-400-deals-worldwide)), "600 job boards database" in the free version and coverage of 146 countries ([Onrec](https://onrec.com/node/109476)), and "over 993 job boards in over 244 countries" on its German page ([Jobboard Finder DE](https://www.jobboardfinder.com/de/worldwide-job-portal)).
- A vendor glossary claims "50,000+ job boards operating globally (Jobboard Finder, 2024)". I could not verify this against Jobboard Finder and it is an outlier. Treat it as unreliable. - [Hyring glossary](https://hyring.com/free-hr-toolkit/hr-glossary/job-board)
- Broadbean (a multiposter) claims a network of "over 7,000 job boards, search engines, and social media channels" in "over 100 countries". - [Broadbean job distribution](https://www.broadbean.com/product-suite/job-distribution-platform/). Partner MHR cites "over 5,000 job boards across 187 countries" via Broadbean. - [MHR](https://mhrglobal.com/uk/en/partners/broadbean)
- Greenhouse's integrations directory reportedly listed 452 partners on 19 October 2026 (third-party count), and Greenhouse has referred to reach across "1,000+" large and niche job boards via Job Ad Market. - [Pin: best Greenhouse integrations](https://www.pin.com/blog/best-greenhouse-integrations/); [Greenhouse integrations](https://greenhouse.com/integrations); [Greenhouse 250 partners blog](https://www.greenhouse.com/blog/surpassing-250-distinct-integration-partners-a-word-from-greenhouses-director-of-partnerships)

**ATS vendors**
- No total vendor count was found. A February 2026 Research and Markets report on the US ATS market covers about 25 vendors, and TechTarget's 2026 roundup profiles 16. - [Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/report/united-states-applicant-tracking-system-market); [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/enterprise-software/tip/16-top-applicant-tracking-systems-for-2026)
- Pin's June 2026 data across 600+ recruiting teams found no ATS above 16.5% share and the top four holding 58.7%, which suggests a long tail. This is one platform's sample. - [Pin ATS market share report](https://www.pin.com/blog/ats-market-share-report)
- Apps Run The World keeps a customer database for recruiting software vendors (commercial), which could be used to size the vendor long tail. - [Apps Run The World recruiting](https://www.appsruntheworld.com/customers-database/category/recruiting/records/desc/10/40)

**Multiposting and programmatic platforms**
- I found no source that counts multiposting or programmatic vendors. Size has to be inferred from ATS marketplaces' "job distribution" categories, for example Greenhouse's job distribution category spans several pages. - [Greenhouse job distribution integrations](https://greenhouse.com/integrations/job-distribution/p7)

**Staffing firms**
- SIA's Largest Global Staffing Firms 2026 list ranks the top 100 firms, which generated $265 billion (EUR 227 billion) in 2025 and held 40% of the global market. Randstad ranked first for the eighth year, followed by Adecco Group, ManpowerGroup and Allegis Group (1.8% share). - [SIA news](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/largest-global-staffing-firms-represent-40-of-market)
- The 2025 edition said the top 100 generated $257 billion in 2024, about 41% of the market. - [Medical Solutions press release](https://www.medicalsolutions.com/blog/news-announcements/medical-solutions-named-among-top-30-largest-staffing-firms-in-the-world/)
- SIA also publishes Largest US Staffing Firms, Largest IT Staffing Firms and Largest Direct Hire Staffing Firms lists. Full reports are for members, but the ranked firms widely publish their placements in press releases. - [Beacon Hill US list](https://bhsg.com/news/beacon-hill-named-to-sias-list-of-largest-u-s-staffing-firms-for-10th-time-ranking-25); [SIA IT staffing list](https://www.staffingindustry.com/editorial/it-staffing-report/list-of-largest-it-staffing-firms-has-fewer-companies); [Beacon Hill direct hire](https://bhsg.com/news/beacon-hill-named-among-the-largest-direct-hire-staffing-firms-globally-for-2025)
- The American Staffing Association says that in 2021 there were about 27,000 staffing and recruiting companies in the US operating close to 54,000 offices (pre-pandemic: about 25,000 companies and 49,000 offices). - [ASA staffing industry statistics](https://americanstaffing.net/staffing-research-data/fact-sheets-analysis-staffing-industry-trends/staffing-industry-statistics/)
- ASA state fact sheets give 2023 office counts from US Census County Business Patterns (for example, Iowa about 330 offices). - [ASA 2025 State Fact Sheets](https://d2m21dzi54s7kp.cloudfront.net/wp-content/uploads/2019/10/ASA-2025StateFactSheets_REV_1-16.pdf)
- The World Employment Confederation is described by ASA as federations "in 50 countries" plus seven large corporate members; WEC allows normally only one national association per country. - [ASA on WEC](https://americanstaffing.net/legal-2/world-employment-confederation/); [WEC global membership](https://wecglobal.org/membership-global/)

**Outplacement and career transition**
- Market size estimates differ widely: US$2,205 million in 2025 ([GlobalInfoResearch via MarketResearch.com](https://www.marketresearch.com/GlobalInfoResearch-v4117/Global-Career-Transition-Outplacement-Company-41334946)) versus about US$10.3 billion in 2024 ([DataHorizzon](https://datahorizzonresearch.com/global-outplacement-services-market-50925)).
- IBISWorld describes US outplacement as highly concentrated, with a few large global firms dominating enterprise accounts. - [IBISWorld](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/procurement/outplacement-services/53933870)
- Everest Group's PEAK Matrix assesses 16 outplacement and career transition providers, which is a usable shortlist of the global tier. - [Everest Group](https://www2.everestgrp.com/reports/EGR-2022-24-R-5712)

**Public employment services**
- WAPES members are national public bodies responsible for employment management. A member agency (Azerbaijan) says WAPES spans more than 80 countries. - [WAPES organisation](https://wapes.org/our-organization/?lang=en); [Azerbaijan SEA](https://www.sosial.gov.az/en/media/news/state-employment-agency-elected-as-a-member-of-the-board-of-directors-of-the-world-association-of-public-employment-services-7992)
- The 2026 World of Public Employment Services report (IDB, OECD, WAPES) surveyed 78 PES in 75 countries. - [WAPES news, June 2026](https://wapes.org/blog/2026/06/09/public-employment-services-urged-to-boost-response-to-labour-market-shocks-through-better-delivery-capacity-say-idb-oecd-and-wapes/?lang=en)
- EURES covers all EU countries plus Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway and Switzerland; each of 31 participating countries designates a National Coordination Office, generally linked to its PES. A European Parliament document counted 219 Members and Partners (around 2021). - [EURES members and partners](https://eures.europa.eu/eures-services/eures-members-and-partners-your-country_en); [ELA](https://www.ela.europa.eu/sq/node/975); [IPEX document](https://secure.ipex.eu/IPEXL-WEB/download/file/082d29087c0cba3e017c0da4215a198b)

### Inferences
- Realistic "complete" targets: job boards 3,000 to 7,000 globally (the multiposter network sizes are the best upper bound for boards that actually take paid postings); public employment services about 80 to 100 national bodies plus regional ones (Belgium, Germany, Spain have regional PES), which is a closed, finishable set; global staffing top 100 (finishable); outplacement global tier about 15 to 20 providers (finishable); ATS a few hundred vendors with a long tail.
- Staffing firms are the one category where full coverage is impossible (27,000 in the US alone). The directory should define a cutoff, for example SIA ranked lists plus WEC and national association corporate members.
- Multiposter network counts include search engines and social channels, so they overstate pure job boards.

### Gaps
- No primary source gives a current global job board count with a stated method. Jobboard Finder's current totals were not verifiable from undated pages.
- No count of multiposting or programmatic vendors was found.
- WAPES's exact membership number: the 2025 annual report text I extracted did not state a member count.
- No reliable global count of outplacement providers or national staffing firm counts outside the US was found within the call budget.

## 2. Open or reusable sources for seeding entries, with licences

### Takeaway
Wikidata is legally the easiest seed (CC0) but is thin: only about 101 items are typed as employment websites. The highest yield per unit of effort comes from closed, authoritative lists (WAPES, EURES, WEC members, SIA ranked lists via firms' own press releases) and from relationship sources (ATS integration marketplaces and multiposter publisher lists), which give names and URLs to verify on each organisation's own site.

### Cited Findings
- Live Wikidata Query Service counts run on 2026-11-12 (`?i wdt:P31 ?class`): employment website (Q580148) 101 items; employment agency (Q261362) 91; temporary work agency (Q59545) 64; recruitment agency (Q17175443) 58; executive search firm (Q63349565) 28; human resource management system (Q2072225) 18; public employment service (Q1690950) 11; applicant tracking system (Q4781462) 7; outplacement (Q1507953) 0 instances. - [Wikidata Query Service](https://query.wikidata.org/)
- Of the 101 employment website items (including subclasses), 97 have an official website (P856) but only 30 have an owner (P127) or parent organisation (P749). Using industry (P452) instead of instance-of adds a few more: employment agency 26, employment website 16, temporary work agency 8, recruitment agency 5. - [Wikidata Query Service](https://query.wikidata.org/)
- Wikidata structured data is released under CC0, so it can be reused without attribution. Wikipedia text is CC BY-SA 4.0, which requires attribution and share-alike for copied text (facts themselves are not copyrightable, but copied prose is). - [Wikidata:Licensing](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Wikidata:Licensing); [Wikipedia:Copyrights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wikipedia:Copyrights)
- The SPARQL endpoint is query.wikidata.org, and subclass-aware queries use `wdt:P31/wdt:P279*`. - [Wikidata Query Service manual](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Wikidata_Query_Service/User_Manual)
- The Wikimedia Action API rate-limited my unauthenticated search requests ("You are making too many requests"), so bulk work should use SPARQL or the dumps and follow Wikimedia's rate limit guidance. - [Wikimedia API rate limits](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Wikimedia_APIs/Rate_limits)
- EURES publishes a per-country list of Members and Partners (PES plus private employment services). - [EURES members and partners in your country](https://eures.europa.eu/eures-services/eures-members-and-partners-your-country_en)
- WEC publishes national federation members by country and its corporate members. - [WEC global membership](https://wecglobal.org/membership-global/); [WEC Europe membership](https://wecglobal.org/membership-europe/); [WEC corporate membership](https://wecglobal.org/membership/corporate-membership/)
- Greenhouse's help centre lists its free job board integrations (for example Appcast, Arbeitnow, Blind, Indeed, Levels.fyi, Monster, ZipRecruiter) and a list of configurable job board partners; its integrations index is organised by category including job distribution. - [Greenhouse free job posts](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/4405801275931-Free-job-posts); [Greenhouse integrations](https://greenhouse.com/integrations)
- OpenCorporates data is under ODbL (share-alike, attribution); free API keys are for open data projects that publish under an open licence and must show evidence of use; otherwise a paid non-share-alike licence is needed. - [OpenCorporates API reference](https://api.opencorporates.com/documentation/API-Reference); [OpenCorporates terms](https://opencorporates.com/terms-of-use-2/); [ODbL summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Database_License)
- SIA full rankings are for paying members, but ranked firms publicise their position (for example Beacon Hill No. 60, Medical Solutions No. 30 on the 2026 global list). - [SIA news](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/largest-global-staffing-firms-represent-40-of-market); [Beacon Hill 2026](https://bhsg.com/news/beacon-hill-named-one-of-the-worlds-largest-staffing-firms-for-10th-time)

### Inferences
- Recommended seed order by yield per effort: (1) WAPES/EURES/OECD lists for PES, a closed set of about 100 with official government sites that rarely block bots; (2) WEC national federations, then each federation's own member directory (for example ASA) for staffing; (3) SIA ranked firms via their own press releases; (4) ATS marketplaces and multiposter publisher lists for job boards, programmatic platforms and ATS-to-board relationships; (5) Wikidata as a cross-check and ID source (QIDs make good stable identifiers) rather than a primary seed.
- Treat association directories and vendor marketplaces as discovery sources only. Copying their compiled lists wholesale may raise database right issues in the EU (sui generis database right) and their terms of use; recording the fact of membership with a link and then verifying on the organisation's own site fits the project's existing quote-based rule. This is an inference, not legal advice.
- Job Board Atlas could contribute back to Wikidata (adding P127/P749 ownership with references), since only 30 of 101 job site items have owners. Contributions to Wikidata become CC0.
- ODbL data from OpenCorporates would impose share-alike on any derived database, so use it for lookup and verification links rather than bulk import unless the atlas is itself ODbL-compatible.

### Gaps
- I did not check licences of individual national staffing association directories (ASA, Recruitment and Employment Confederation, Prism'emploi, etc.); most are likely all-rights-reserved web pages.
- IAEWS member directory content and terms were not verified within the call budget.
- Lever, Workable, SmartRecruiters and other ATS marketplaces were not checked individually.

## 3. Data points for comparing outplacement providers and staffing firms, and where they are stated

### Takeaway
The comparable, reliably stated fields are owner/parent, headquarters, countries served, service lines, revenue rank, and membership of a national federation. Pricing is rarely published for outplacement or staffing; size is best taken from SIA ranks or annual reports rather than from company sites.

### Cited Findings
- SIA ranks firms by staffing revenue and reports market share, which gives a size field with a named source and year. - [SIA news](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/largest-global-staffing-firms-represent-40-of-market)
- SIA's segment lists (US, IT, direct hire, global) give a services dimension: a firm on the direct hire list has permanent placement as a material line. - [Beacon Hill direct hire](https://bhsg.com/news/beacon-hill-named-among-the-largest-direct-hire-staffing-firms-globally-for-2025)
- For outplacement, the large global firms are often units of listed groups, so parent company annual reports or filings are the best source of ownership and scale. - [IBISWorld outplacement concentration](https://www.ibisworld.com/united-states/procurement/outplacement-services/53933870)
- Everest Group's PEAK Matrix evaluates outplacement providers on market impact and vision and capability, which shows analyst comparison dimensions (scale, geographic reach, delivery model, technology). - [Everest Group](https://www2.everestgrp.com/reports/EGR-2022-24-R-5712)
- National federation membership (for example via WEC national members) implies adherence to a code of conduct, which is a useful trust field. - [WEC national federation membership](https://wecglobal.org/membership/national-federation-membership/)

### Inferences
- Suggested core schema for staffing and outplacement: legal name, brand, parent/ultimate owner (with source and date), HQ country, countries with offices, service lines (temporary, permanent, executive search, RPO, MSP, outplacement, career coaching, redeployment), delivery model (in person, virtual, hybrid), sector specialisms, federation memberships, size band (SIA rank, revenue band, or headcount from annual report), founding year, lifecycle events. Offices and countries are usually stated on "locations" pages; services on service pages.
- Pricing models: outplacement providers tend to sell per-participant packages by seniority and duration, but published prices are rare. Record "pricing published: yes/no" plus model type only where quoted on the site, rather than leaving a field that will mostly be empty.

### Gaps
- No open source states outplacement pricing at scale; I found no reliable public data.
- I did not confirm the named member list of the Everest PEAK Matrix (paywalled).

## 4. How other open data projects handle verification, freshness and corrections

### Takeaway
Mature projects separate the claim from its source and its check date. OpenStreetMap records when a fact was last checked (check_date, including per-tag variants); Wikidata attaches references and ranks to each statement; Open Ownership's BODS makes every ownership statement carry source, publication details and annotations for changes; OpenCorporates links each record back to the official register. Job Board Atlas can transfer per-field check dates, statement-level provenance, and append-only ownership statements.

### Cited Findings
- OSM `check_date` records the date of the latest review of an object (YYYY-MM-DD); `check_date:<key>` records when a single tag was checked (for example collection_times); it is also used for partial surveys; and there is no full consensus, with alternatives such as `last_checked:date` in use. - [OSM wiki Key:check_date](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:check_date); [OSM wiki Key:check_date:*](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/Key:check_date:*)
- OSM `check_date:poi:*` lets mappers record that they confirmed the absence of a feature on a date, a model for recording "confirmed closed" or "no longer offers X". - [OSM wiki Key:check_date:poi](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Key:check_date:poi)
- BODS: each statement should contain information on its source and whether it was verified; `publicationDetails` (publication date, BODS version, publisher) is required; and systems that clean or reconcile data should record changes as annotations so provenance is traceable. - [BODS schema reference](https://beneficial-ownership-data-standard.readthedocs.io/en/latest/schema/reference.html); [BODS provenance guidance v0.1](https://beneficial-ownership-data-standard.readthedocs.io/en/v0-1/schema/guidance/provenance.html); [BODS examples](https://beneficial-ownership-data-standard.readthedocs.io/en/latest/examples/index.html)
- OpenCorporates uses each object's OpenCorporates URL as the attribution link and provides an OpenRefine reconciliation API for matching names to company records. - [OpenCorporates API reference](https://api.opencorporates.com/documentation/API-Reference); [OpenCorporates reconciliation API](https://api.opencorporates.com/documentation/Open-Refine-Reconciliation-API)

### Inferences
- Transferable practices:
  - Per-field `checked_on` dates (OSM style) rather than one record-level date, so ownership can be re-checked on a different cycle from description text.
  - Statement-level ownership records (BODS style): subject, interested party, start date, end date, source URL, quote, retrieval date. Acquisitions and closures become dated statements that are superseded, not overwritten, which matches the existing lifecycle log.
  - Explicit "verified absence" or "closed" states with a date and evidence, mirroring check_date:poi.
  - Correction requests as a public queue (for example a GitHub issue template or form) with a required source URL, processed by the same quote-verification rule.
  - Risk-based re-check cadence: fast-changing fields (ownership, status, pricing) every 6 to 12 months and after news of mergers; slow fields (founding year, HQ) on demand. This cadence is a suggestion, not drawn from a cited project rule.
  - Stable external identifiers: Wikidata QID, OpenCorporates URL, LEI or SEC CIK where they exist, to deduplicate and to detect renames.
- Using Wikidata statements with references as a second opinion can flag discrepancies cheaply, given only a minority of items carry owner data.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch Wikidata's own documentation on references, ranks and the "deprecated" rank within the call budget; the description of Wikidata practices above relies on general knowledge and should be confirmed at [Help:Ranking](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Ranking) and [Help:Sources](https://www.wikidata.org/wiki/Help:Sources).
- No statistics on correction volumes or re-check cycles at OSM or OpenCorporates were gathered.

## 5. Handling sites that block automated reading, while respecting site rules

### Takeaway
When an organisation's own site returns 403 or a Cloudflare challenge, the best compliant fallbacks are: archived copies (Wayback availability and CDX APIs, Common Crawl index), the organisation's own press releases on newswires, regulatory filings (SEC EDGAR via its free JSON APIs, company registries, annual reports) for ownership, and a small queued manual browser pass for the remainder. Do not try to defeat bot protection.

### Cited Findings
- Wayback Machine availability API (`archive.org/wayback/available`) returns the closest capture to a timestamp; the CDX API (`web.archive.org/cdx/search/cdx`) lists all captures with timestamp, status code, MIME type and digest. One guide reports the availability API sometimes returns empty for archived URLs, so fall back to CDX. - [Internet Archive Wayback API](https://archive.org/help/wayback_api.php); [TDS guide on Wayback and Common Crawl](https://tds.s-anand.net/2026-02/docs/week-6/wayback-commoncrawl/)
- Common Crawl's index (`index.commoncrawl.org`, per crawl ID from collinfo.json) gives page text but no replay; it is useful for bulk extraction and pages Wayback missed. The cdx_toolkit library queries both. - [TDS guide](https://tds.s-anand.net/2026-02/docs/week-6/wayback-commoncrawl/); [Bright Data comparison](https://brightdata.com/blog/web-data/internet-archive-vs-common-crawl-vs-web-archive)
- SEC EDGAR offers free JSON APIs on data.sec.gov (submissions and XBRL data), with a maximum of 10 requests per second per user across machines, and requires a User-Agent declaring a company name and contact email; excessive requesters may be blocked. - [SEC Accessing EDGAR Data](https://www.sec.gov/os/accessing-edgar-data); [SEC developer resources](https://www.sec.gov/developer)
- Ranked staffing firms publish their own press releases about rankings (for example PrideStaff, Addison Group, Insight Global), which are first-party statements often hosted on newswires as well as the firm's site. - [PrideStaff press room](https://www.pridestaff.com/press-room/pridestaff-named-to-staffing-industry-analysts-list-of-largest-u-s-staffing-firms-for-13th-consecutive-year); [Addison Group on Newswire](https://www.newswire.com/news/addison-group-named-one-of-the-largest-staffing-firms-in-the-u-s-for-22600217)
- OpenCorporates and BODS-based registers provide registry-backed ownership and company records that can be cited instead of a blocked site. - [OpenCorporates API reference](https://api.opencorporates.com/documentation/API-Reference); [BODS schema reference](https://beneficial-ownership-data-standard.readthedocs.io/en/latest/schema/reference.html)

### Inferences
- Suggested fallback ladder for a research agent: (1) own site, respecting robots.txt; (2) other first-party pages on different hosts (investor relations subdomain, help centre, careers site, press room on a newswire); (3) the latest Wayback capture, recording the capture timestamp as the evidence date; (4) regulatory sources for ownership and lifecycle (SEC 8-K and 10-K, UK Companies House, annual reports); (5) Wikidata/Wikipedia as pointers only; (6) a weekly manual browser batch for the remainder, with the human pasting the quote.
- Evidence from an archived copy should be marked as such (source type "archive", capture date) so freshness checks can prioritise it for live re-verification.
- For an agent that cannot set a contact email, EDGAR access may be refused; this needs a project contact address in the User-Agent, which is a project decision.
- Large listed parents (Randstad, Adecco, ManpowerGroup, Recruit, SEEK, Stepstone's owner) publish annual reports listing subsidiaries and acquisitions, which can cover many blocked brands in one document. This is inference; individual reports were not checked.

### Gaps
- I did not verify Wayback or Common Crawl terms of use for reuse of quotes, or official Common Crawl documentation (sources used are third-party guides).
- No source was checked for non-US filing APIs (for example Companies House API, EU BRIS) within the call budget.
