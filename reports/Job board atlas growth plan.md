# Answer integration questions to grow the Atlas

Job Board Atlas should put its next effort into depth, not more pages. People already reach the site by asking how ATS products connect to job boards, which ones have a public job posting API, and who handles job multiposting. The site's answers to those questions are thin. Structured API details exist for only **16 of 607 entries**. **17 of the 23 multiposting and distribution entries have zero facts.** No page tells a reader which multiposters serve a given ATS ([project audit](/home/user/job-board-list/research_notes/Job%20board%20atlas%20growth%20plan/project_audit.md)). The plan below scores 17 options on a stated 20-point scale. The top items are an integration and API layer, a quick pass to verify and publish five well-known entries that are hidden today (Broadbean, Pracuj and others), Bing and IndexNow setup, a deeper multiposting and distribution section, and a trust layer (methodology, check dates, corrections). The competitor directory goal has a clear opening because Jobboardfinder, the largest neutral job board encyclopedia (993 boards, 244 countries), **closed in 2026** ([Jobboardfinder](https://www.jobboardfinder.com/)). Staffing, though, needs a defined cutoff, because the US alone has about **27,000 staffing firms** ([ASA](https://americanstaffing.net/staffing-research-data/fact-sheets-analysis-staffing-industry-trends/staffing-industry-statistics/)). Three tempting moves score lowest and are not recommended: lowering the indexing bar so thin pages get indexed, doing more work on files written for AI tools, and selling paid placements. The site is new, so results should be judged over 3 to 6 months, not week by week.

## Searchers ask about integrations, and the site barely answers

In its first week of Search Console data (owner's screenshots, 11 November 2026) the site had **2 clicks, 246 impressions and an average position of 32.4**. The top page was /tags/ats-integration/ and the top query was "ats job board integration" ([project audit](/home/user/job-board-list/research_notes/Job%20board%20atlas%20growth%20plan/project_audit.md)). The other queries follow the same pattern: "job multiposter & distribution for lever", "jobvite integration", "recruitee api", "stepstone api", "greenhouse api", "which ats have a public api for job postings" and "job feed management services in uk". These are factual, technical questions. A cited directory can answer them better than anyone else, and today it mostly doesn't. Lever has 19 documented connections and 7 API detail rows, yet its page never mentions multiposting. StepStone, Jobvite, Handshake, CV-Library, France Travail, Indeed and Taleo have no structured API details at all ([data/api_details.csv](/home/user/job-board-list/data/api_details.csv); [docs/boards/lever/index.html](/home/user/job-board-list/docs/boards/lever/index.html)).

Outside research points the same way. The most underserved audiences are talent acquisition operations teams, staffing agencies, ATS vendors and developers. External changes keep forcing them to rewire how jobs get posted. Indeed stopped taking new single-source XML feeds from integrated ATS partners in the US in June 2025 ([College Recruiter](https://www.collegerecruiter.com/blog/2025/06/19/breaking-news-indeed-wont-accept-jobs-via-xml-ep106)), and it set a **31 March 2026** single-source feed cut-off that SAP told its own customers to prepare for ([Indeed help centre](https://au.indeed.com/help/employers/articles/single-source-feed-policy-effective-march-31-2026); [SAP KBA 3710318](https://userapps.support.sap.com/sap/support/knowledge/en/3710318)). Integration lists do exist, but each vendor publishes only its own: Indeed lists its ATS partners, and each ATS help centre lists its own boards ([Indeed docs](https://docs.indeed.com/sponsored-jobs-api/ats-integrations/); [Access Recruit help](https://help-accessrecruit.theaccessgroup.com/en/articles/13616405-job-boards-that-integrate-with-access-recruit)). Multiposters advertise network sizes instead of named lists. Broadbean claims 7,000+ boards in one place and 5,000+ in another ([Broadbean](https://www.broadbean.com/product-suite/job-distribution-platform/); [MHR](https://mhrglobal.com/uk/en/partners/broadbean)). Nobody publishes a cited list that covers all vendors. Compliance questions add steady demand. US federal contractors must still list most openings with state employment services under VEVRAA ([DOL eLaws](https://webapps.dol.gov/elaws/elg/vietvets.htm)). The query "ofccp job multiposter" already brings impressions, but the site has no OFCCP tag or page.

The data model can already hold these answers, because the relationships, API details and ingest-method fields exist. What's missing is coverage. The relationship graph has **202 edges**, and only 52 of its endpoints are entries in the directory. Another **114 endpoints don't match any entry**, and the research queue holds **105 partner names** that other companies list on their integration pages but that aren't yet in the directory ([data/relationships.csv](/home/user/job-board-list/data/relationships.csv); [data/research_queue.csv](/home/user/job-board-list/data/research_queue.csv)). That backlog is the quickest way to add the ATS products and multiposters that searchers name.

## Five strong pages are hidden, and a few technical leaks waste ranking signals

The site keeps a board page out of search results unless it has at least 3 cited facts and a confidence level of partially-verified or secondary-sourced. Only **193 of 607 boards pass**, which is close to the 204 pages Google reports as indexed. That makes the rule, not crawling, the limit on how many pages are indexed ([scripts/build_site.py](/home/user/job-board-list/scripts/build_site.py)). The rule is sound, but it is blunt. Broadbean (7 facts), Pracuj (7), SuccessFactors (5), Taleo (3) and GulfTalent (3) are hidden only because their confidence is still "seed-unverified". Pracuj is the leading Polish board, and "polish job boards" was the second-largest query. Verifying these five entries is a few hours of work, and each matches a query people are already typing.

Several smaller technical problems spread ranking signals across the wrong URLs. The 21 /ats/ and 55 language-alias pages are not real redirects. They return a normal page that forwards the visitor (a meta refresh), and Google still shows /ats/lever/ in results (15 impressions). The raw job_boards.csv download drew 15 impressions, more than most HTML pages. www.jobboardlist.com redirects through http before it reaches https. Sixteen country pages are titled with ISO codes such as "bg" or "zz" ([project audit](/home/user/job-board-list/research_notes/Job%20board%20atlas%20growth%20plan/project_audit.md)). GitHub Pages can't send real redirects or noindex headers, but rules at Cloudflare can fix each of these. The home page is **543,827 bytes with about 11,700 words**, because the whole table loads at once. That is below Googlebot's 2 MB HTML cutoff ([Search Engine Journal](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/google-updates-googlebot-file-size-limit-docs)), but it is heavy on phones and blurs what the home page is about. A lighter home page with links to the main hubs, plus a paginated full list, would serve both readers and search engines better.

The research pipeline also needs redirecting. Refine rounds now add 7 to 12 facts each, down from 25 in round 1, and they mostly fill generic fields such as founding year and headquarters. About 18% of reviewed sites block automated reading ([data/loop_log.csv](/home/user/job-board-list/data/loop_log.csv); [data/reviews.csv](/home/user/job-board-list/data/reviews.csv)). That effort should go to API, integration and ownership facts, with a rule-respecting fallback for blocked sites. The fallback order is: other first-party pages, Wayback captures (recorded as archive evidence) ([Internet Archive](https://archive.org/help/wayback_api.php)), regulatory filings ([SEC EDGAR](https://www.sec.gov/os/accessing-edgar-data)), and a small manual batch.

## Search engines reward cited depth, not extra files or more thin pages

Google doesn't ban pages built from a database. Its spam policy targets pages made "for the primary purpose of manipulating search rankings and not helping users", including stitched-together content that adds no value ([Google spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)). Its May 2026 guide on generative search features says that producing many pages aimed at AI answers can breach the same policy. It also says a site needs no special files, markup or Markdown to appear in those answers, and that Google Search ignores llms.txt ([Google guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)). A large June 2026 study found that **97% of published llms.txt files received zero requests** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/llmstxt-study/)). The site's llms.txt, Markdown twins and JSON API cost little to keep and help developers. They shouldn't get more effort, and the Markdown copies should be marked noindex so they don't compete with the HTML pages. FAQ rich results stopped showing for all sites on **7 May 2026** ([TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/ai-search/google-faq-rich-results-deprecated-2026)), so the FAQPage markup on 608 pages now helps only as plain question-and-answer text, not as a display feature.

The part of AI search the site can influence is long-tail answers. Google says its AI features run several related searches on subtopics and show "a wider and more diverse set of helpful links" ([Google AI features](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). An industry analysis of 4 million cited URLs found **only 37.9% of cited pages ranked in the top 10**, and 31% ranked beyond position 100 ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-citations-top-10/)). A new domain can therefore be cited before it ranks well, if a page states a specific answer plainly, for example: "Does X have a public job posting API? Yes, REST, here are the docs, verified on this date." Bing Webmaster Tools now reports how often Bing's AI answers cite your pages and which background queries triggered them ([Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/bing-webmaster-tools-testing-new-ai-performance-report-468039)). IndexNow pushes changed URLs to Bing and several other engines, but not Google ([IndexNow](https://www.indexnow.org/faq)). Both are cheap to set up.

Set expectations carefully. In a sample of one million URLs, only **1.74% of new pages reached Google's top 10 within a year** ([Ahrefs](https://ahrefs.com/blog/how-long-does-it-take-to-rank/)). An average position of 32 after one week is a starting point, not a verdict. Links matter for a new domain. Google warns that chasing inauthentic mentions does little ([Google guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/ai-optimization-guide)). Practitioners consistently report that original, citable data earns links ([Backlinko](https://backlinko.com/hub/seo/original-research)), but no controlled study measures how many. Data reports drawn from the dataset, such as "who owns which job boards" or "which ATS have open posting APIs", are the site's natural source of links. Paid third-party placements are the pattern Google's site reputation policy describes, so they should stay off the domain.

## Jobboardfinder's exit leaves the neutral directory seat empty

Each competitor covers only a slice. Jobboardfinder, owned by an HR tech group with its own ATS and agency, now shows a closure notice ([Jobboardfinder](https://www.jobboardfinder.com/)). Onrec's directory is large and UK-leaning, with bare listings and paid plans ([Onrec](https://www.onrec.com/directory/job-boards)). Wikipedia's list has about **51 rows and only two references** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_employment_websites)). Talent Tech Labs maps 500+ vendors only as infographics ([hrtechfeed](https://hrtechfeed.com/?p=434079)). ATS marketplaces list only their own partners ([Greenhouse integrations](https://greenhouse.com/integrations)). None of them publishes a citation for each fact, a last-checked date, an open API, or a graph that links boards, ATS products, multiposters, staffing firms and owners. Job Board Atlas has all of these. Positioning it as the open, cited successor to Jobboardfinder is credible, and the boards Jobboardfinder used to list are a natural group to contact.

Outplacement is the most open niche. Search results for outplacement firm lists are dominated by vendor listicles that rank their own authors first ([Careerminds](https://careerminds.com/top-outplacement-firms-list); [IMPACT Group](https://impactgrouphr.com/blog/best-outplacement-firms)). The field is a closed, finishable set: Everest Group assesses **16 providers** ([Everest Group](https://www2.everestgrp.com/reports/EGR-2022-24-R-5712)), and the leaders belong to staffing groups (LHH under Adecco, Right Management under ManpowerGroup, RiseSmart under Randstad, INTOO under Gi Group) ([Mordor Intelligence](https://www.mordorintelligence.com/industry-reports/outplacement-market)). The atlas has only 9 outplacement, 9 career services and 27 staffing entries. Most are thin because the fact score is built for job boards: API status and ingest methods mean nothing for an outplacement firm ([project audit](/home/user/job-board-list/research_notes/Job%20board%20atlas%20growth%20plan/project_audit.md)). These categories need their own fact set: owner, countries, service lines, delivery model, levels served, federation membership, size band, and whether pricing is published.

Scope rules keep the directory honest. Job boards number about 3,500 worldwide by an older industry estimate ([SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/Research/Research-Reports/APAC/Online-Job-Advertising-Market)). Public employment services are a closed set of about 78 to 100, reachable through WAPES and EURES ([WAPES](https://wapes.org/blog/2026/06/09/public-employment-services-urged-to-boost-response-to-labour-market-shocks-through-better-delivery-capacity-say-idb-oecd-and-wapes/?lang=en); [EURES](https://eures.europa.eu/eures-services/eures-members-and-partners-your-country_en)). The top 100 global staffing firms hold 40% of the market ([SIA](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/largest-global-staffing-firms-represent-40-of-market)), so that list makes a sensible staffing cutoff. For seed data, Wikidata is CC0 but thin (101 employment-website items, only 30 with an owner). OpenCorporates data is ODbL, which would impose share-alike terms on any database built from it ([OpenCorporates terms](https://opencorporates.com/terms-of-use-2/)). Association lists and vendor marketplaces should be used to find names, then verified on each organisation's own site, rather than copied. Ownership also needs a cleanup. The data holds **121 parent-company spellings for 21 profiled owners**, and only 19 of 112 owner pages are indexable. One owner appears as "Recruit Holdings Co., Ltd.", "Recruit Holdings" and "Recruit Co. Ltd." ([data/job_boards.csv](/home/user/job-board-list/data/job_boards.csv)). Recording ownership as dated statements with sources, as the Beneficial Ownership Data Standard does ([BODS](https://beneficial-ownership-data-standard.readthedocs.io/en/latest/schema/reference.html)), would support an M&A timeline that investors and board operators currently can only get from paid reports.

## Scored options: integration depth leads, mass indexing trails

**Scoring method.** Each option gets four scores from 1 to 5, where higher is always better. **Value** is how much the option helps people discover and understand the hiring ecosystem, or helps build the competitor directory. **Search** is the expected effect on Google, Bing and AI-answer visibility. **Effort** is reversed, so 5 means a few hours and 1 means months of work. **Risk** is also reversed, so 5 means little chance of a quality, legal or ranking problem. The four scores are added with equal weight to give a total out of 20. Ties are broken by Value. The scores are judgment calls based on the evidence above, not measurements.

| Rank | Option | Value | Search | Effort | Risk | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Verify and publish the five high-demand hidden entries (Broadbean, Pracuj, SuccessFactors, Taleo, GulfTalent) | 4 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 18 |
| 2 | Integration and API layer: a page per major ATS (which boards and multiposters, by what method), a "which ATS have a public job posting API" comparison, and API details for the queried entities | 5 | 5 | 3 | 4 | 17 |
| 3 | Deepen multiposting and distribution: fill the 17 zero-fact entries, add LogicMelon and Bullhorn, add a UK feed management page and an OFCCP/VEVRAA posting tag | 5 | 4 | 3 | 4 | 16 |
| 4 | Trust layer: methodology page, check date for each field, public corrections form, named maintainer | 4 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 16 |
| 5 | Clean up owners and build an M&A and closures timeline with dated, sourced statements | 4 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 16 |
| 6 | Bing Webmaster Tools, IndexNow on deploy, check that Cloudflare doesn't block search and AI crawlers, track AI referrals | 2 | 4 | 5 | 5 | 16 |
| 7 | Technical cleanup at Cloudflare: real redirects for the 76 stubs, one-hop www redirect, noindex headers on CSV and Markdown files, fix ISO-code country pages | 2 | 3 | 5 | 5 | 15 |
| 8 | Public employment services as a complete set (WAPES and EURES seed, about 80 to 100 bodies) | 3 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 15 |
| 9 | Work the relationship backlog (105 queued partners, 114 unmatched endpoints) | 4 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 14 |
| 10 | Open-successor positioning plus outreach to listed organisations and data reports for links | 4 | 4 | 3 | 3 | 14 |
| 11 | Separate fact set and directory sections for outplacement and staffing, with a stated cutoff | 5 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 13 |
| 12 | Lighter home page with a paginated full list | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 13 |
| 13 | Move research effort to integration facts, with a fallback for blocked sites | 3 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 13 |
| 14 | Contribute ownership statements to Wikidata and list the dataset on open data portals | 2 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 13 |
| 15 | Lower the 3-fact bar to index more thin pages | 2 | 2 | 5 | 1 | 10 |
| 16 | More work on llms.txt and AI-only files | 1 | 1 | 4 | 4 | 10 |

**Sequencing.** In the first 30 days, do rows 1, 6 and 7 (mostly settings and verification) and start row 2 with Lever, Greenhouse, Workable, iCIMS, Jobvite and StepStone, the entities already getting impressions. From day 30 to 90, finish rows 2 and 3, publish the trust layer and owner cleanup (rows 4 and 5), and start the public employment services set. From day 90 to 180, build the outplacement and staffing sections, publish the first data report, and start outreach. Progress should be checked weekly for indexing and errors, monthly for queries, and quarterly for decisions. Early signs are the share of sitemap URLs indexed by page type, distinct queries, queries ranking better than position 20, Bing AI citations, and dataset downloads. Clicks and links are later signs. Before scaling the outplacement work, the keyword figures need checking in a paid tool. The two free tools found disagree by about four times on "outplacement services" volume and 25 times on cost per click ([SEOJuice](https://seojuice.com/keyword-research/business-services/hr-consulting-&-employment-law/); [iSpionage](https://www.ispionage.com/Competitive_Intelligence_directory/i/145/impactgrouphr_com_696D7061637467726F757068722E636F6D)).

There is a fair case for doing more breadth first: more countries and more boards, to match Jobboardfinder's old 993. But the evidence favours depth. Each impression the site earns today comes from a page with real facts. Google's policies punish volume without value. And a new domain gets cited for specific answers, not for size.

## Conclusion

The atlas's advantage is not its size; it is that each fact has a source and that the facts are linked across boards, ATS products, multiposters and owners. That combination answers the questions forced on recruiters by platform policy changes, and no vendor marketplace or closed directory answers them. Growth comes from turning the existing data model into direct answers on integration, API access and ownership, then widening into outplacement and staffing only with category-specific facts and stated scope rules. The main open question is demand size. Query volumes for integration and multiposting terms are unverified, and one week of Search Console data is noisy. The first quarter's results should decide how far to push the competitor directory against the integration layer.

## For owner approval

1. Verify and publish Broadbean, Pracuj, SuccessFactors, Taleo and GulfTalent (row 1).
2. Build the integration and API layer, starting with six ATS and board pages and a public-API comparison page (row 2).
3. Research and fill the multiposting and distribution section, including a UK feed management page and an OFCCP/VEVRAA tag (row 3).
4. Set up Bing Webmaster Tools and IndexNow, and grant access to review Cloudflare bot settings (row 6).
5. Add Cloudflare rules for real redirects, a one-hop www redirect and noindex headers on CSV and Markdown files, and fix the ISO-code country pages (row 7).
6. Publish a methodology page, field-level check dates and a public corrections form (row 4).
7. Clean up owner names and publish a dated ownership and closures timeline (row 5).
8. Pause general field refinement (founding year, headquarters) in favour of integration and ownership research (row 13).
9. Keep the 3-fact indexing rule, and drop further llms.txt work (rows 15 and 16).

## Decisions only the owner can make

1. **Positioning:** whether to present the site publicly as the open successor to Jobboardfinder and contact the boards it used to list.
2. **Scope:** whether staffing and outplacement are full sections or an appendix, and the staffing cutoff (for example the SIA top 100 plus national federation members).
3. **Money:** whether the site will ever accept paid or claimed profiles. This plan advises against paid placements on the domain.
4. **Identity and contact:** a named maintainer for the About page, and a contact email for the corrections form and for filing-data requests.
5. **Licence:** the dataset licence, and whether to accept share-alike sources such as OpenCorporates.
6. **Budget:** whether to pay for a keyword research tool to confirm demand before the outplacement build.
7. **Wikidata:** whether to contribute ownership data under a project account.
